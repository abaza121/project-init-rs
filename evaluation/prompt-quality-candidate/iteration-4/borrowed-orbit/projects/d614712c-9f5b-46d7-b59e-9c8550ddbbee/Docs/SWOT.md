# Project Lens

## Strengths

- A single hold-and-release action creates a compact, communicable core loop.
- One screen and short runs bound the first prototype.
- Momentum continuity, anchor choice, collectibles, skims, and multiplier maintenance create several mastery surfaces from one input.
- Sparse geometry aligns presentation with solo production.

## Weaknesses

- “Attaches,” “new arc,” “skim,” “orbit multiplier,” and “instantly” need operational tuning.
- The exact physics model and collision response are not approved.
- Animation-and-sound comprehension can be hard to judge before an intended audience exists.
- A one-button premise can still exclude players if hold behavior or modality support is chosen poorly.

## Opportunities

- Deterministic replay can turn motion disputes into inspectable traces.
- A single authored challenge can isolate whether anchor switching is learnable before content expansion.
- Geometric form, color, pulse, trail, and sound can carry state without instructional copy.
- Seeded scenarios could later add controlled variety once its goals are approved.

## Threats

- Unstable or opaque gravity behavior would invalidate the central proof.
- Premature randomness could mask skill development and make runs feel unfair.
- Unsupported performance targets could produce misleading optimization work.
- Treating provisional accessibility experiments as a complete scope could exclude affected players.
- Expanding into content, market, or platform work before movement validation could exhaust solo capacity.
