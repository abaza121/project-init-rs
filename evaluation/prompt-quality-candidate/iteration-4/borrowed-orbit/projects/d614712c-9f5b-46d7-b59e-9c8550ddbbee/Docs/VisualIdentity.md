# Visual System

Status: proposed working direction. The palette, form language, cue mapping, and production limits below are reversible until stakeholder review; only the sparse geometric, solo-producible, animation-and-sound brief boundaries are authoritative.

## Character

Sparse, tense, legible, and kinetic. The world should feel like a precision instrument caught at the edge of collapse, not a detailed astronomical simulation.

## Forms

- Satellite: a bright diamond or compact cross with one unmistakable facing-free silhouette.
- Planets: large circles with a single ring, notch, or orbit line to distinguish anchor classes.
- Debris: angular shards or hollow polygons, visibly smaller and more irregular than planets.
- Data fragments: tiny paired squares or crystalline ticks with a regular pulse.
- Hazards: broken red-orange outlines with a separate skim halo.
- Collapsing star: an off-center concentric disc with inward particles and a slowly intensifying corona.
- Gravity connection: a thin curved tether or radial pulse visible only while attached.

## Palette

Use near-black indigo for space, warm ivory for the satellite, cool cyan for data and safe feedback, violet-blue for gravity state, amber for score opportunity, and coral-red for collision danger. Preserve value contrast without relying on hue alone; pair every critical color with shape, motion, or sound.

## Motion language

Attachment snaps the tether on, compresses the anchor pulse, and bends the satellite trail. Release breaks the tether cleanly while the trail remains continuous. A skim produces a brief tangent spark and rising tone; collision produces a radial break and muted impact. Multiplier health appears as trail segmentation and pulse cadence, not explanatory text.

## Composition

Keep the playfield on one fixed screen with generous negative space. Limit simultaneous decorative particles, keep hazards visually distinct from eligible anchors, and reserve the brightest value for the satellite and immediate action feedback.

## Production limits

Favor code-drawn primitives, reusable shaders or gradients, small procedural particle sets, and a compact sound palette. Avoid detailed textures, character animation, cinematic scenes, large illustration sets, and art that implies unapproved mechanics.
