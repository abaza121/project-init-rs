# Creative Production Prompt

## Prompt

Design key art and an in-game visual frame for “Borrowed Orbit,” a minimalist one-button browser game about a tiny satellite surviving by borrowing gravity from moving planets and debris. Show a single-screen deep-space composition built from sparse geometric primitives. A tiny luminous diamond-shaped satellite arcs around a calm circular anchor, then departs on a clear tangent shown by a thin fading trajectory. Include a few collectible data fragments, angular hazards with readable skim zones, and available objects drifting toward a large collapsing star at the edge of the frame. Convey precision, momentum, danger, and rapid replay without instructional text, characters, realism, dense UI, or painterly detail. Use a near-black background, restrained cool anchor tones, one bright satellite accent, and one warm hazard-and-star accent. Keep every form plausible for a solo developer to reproduce with vector shapes, simple shaders, and procedural motion. The image should feel like a scientific diagram becoming an arcade game.

## Negative Direction

Avoid photorealism, astronauts, cockpit interfaces, paragraphs of UI copy, complex inventory panels, multiple action buttons, ornate spacecraft, crowded star fields, detailed surface textures, cinematic camera angles, and visual effects that hide trajectories or collision boundaries.

## Adaptation Notes

For a logo, reduce the composition to a small satellite tangent leaving a broken orbit ring. For a thumbnail, preserve the satellite, one anchor, one hazard, and the starward pull. For gameplay, prioritize motion readability over poster drama and keep all exact physics and accessibility behavior open until confirmed.
