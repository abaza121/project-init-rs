# Visual and Sonic Identity

## Core Idea

Borrowed Orbit is a sparse instrument panel turned into a space scene. The satellite is the smallest bright mark; gravity objects are calm geometric masses; the collapsing star is the dominant pressure field. Curved motion trails make timing legible and turn each run into a temporary diagram.

## Shape Language

- Satellite: a bright diamond or compact chevron with a single direction tick.
- Planets: circles with one orbit ring, notch, or radial accent to distinguish behavior without texture-heavy art.
- Debris: triangles, bars, and broken polygons with consistent collision silhouettes.
- Data fragments: small bracketed points or paired squares that pulse on approach.
- Hazards: angular silhouettes with a thin, clearly separated skim band.
- Star: concentric discs or polygons with a slow inward breathing rhythm.

## Color Roles

Use a near-black field, one cool satellite color, a restrained neutral range for anchors, one warm hazard color, and a high-energy star accent. Never rely on color alone: attachment, eligibility, collection, skim, multiplier decay, collision, and termination also need changes in shape, motion, brightness, or sound.

## Motion Roles

Candidate anchors may use a subtle range pulse. Attachment should create an immediate geometric tether or curvature cue. Release should preserve the tangent visually through the trail. Starward drift should be visible over time, and multiplier state should alter a repeated rhythmic cue rather than add explanatory copy.

## Sound Roles

Use short, distinct events for attach, release, fragment collection, safe skim, multiplier change, collision, and restart. The audio vocabulary should reinforce animation rather than carry unique information by itself. Exact loudness controls, captions or equivalents, reduced-motion behavior, and alternative input feedback remain unsettled accessibility choices.

## Production Boundary

Prefer reusable vector or procedural primitives, parameterized motion, and a small sound palette. Avoid bespoke character animation, painted backgrounds, long cinematics, or content volumes that conflict with solo-developer capacity.
