# Product Commitments

## Requirement Register

REQ-001: The prototype shall be a minimalist one-button browser game; exact brief clause: “I want a minimalist one-button browser game where a small satellite survives by borrowing gravity from objects moving across the screen.”
REQ-002: The small satellite shall survive by borrowing gravity from objects moving across the screen; exact brief clause: “I want a minimalist one-button browser game where a small satellite survives by borrowing gravity from objects moving across the screen.”
REQ-003: Holding the button shall attach the satellite to the nearest eligible planet or piece of debris; exact brief clause: “Holding the button attaches the satellite to the nearest planet or piece of debris”.
REQ-004: Releasing the button shall preserve the satellite’s momentum and send the player into a new arc; exact brief clause: “releasing it preserves momentum and sends the player into a new arc.”
REQ-005: The player shall be able to collect data fragments; exact brief clause: “The goal is to collect data fragments, skim hazards for bonus points, and keep an orbit multiplier alive while the available objects drift toward a collapsing star.”
REQ-006: The player shall receive bonus points for skimming hazards; exact brief clause: “The goal is to collect data fragments, skim hazards for bonus points, and keep an orbit multiplier alive while the available objects drift toward a collapsing star.”
REQ-007: The game shall provide an orbit multiplier that the player can keep alive; exact brief clause: “The goal is to collect data fragments, skim hazards for bonus points, and keep an orbit multiplier alive while the available objects drift toward a collapsing star.”
REQ-008: Available gravity objects shall drift toward a collapsing star; exact brief clause: “The goal is to collect data fragments, skim hazards for bonus points, and keep an orbit multiplier alive while the available objects drift toward a collapsing star.”
REQ-009: Every run shall end in less than three minutes; exact brief clause: “Runs should take less than three minutes, restart instantly, and be understandable from animation and sound instead of text.”
REQ-010: A completed or failed run shall restart instantly; exact brief clause: “Runs should take less than three minutes, restart instantly, and be understandable from animation and sound instead of text.”
REQ-011: Gameplay shall be understandable from animation and sound instead of visible instructional text; exact brief clause: “Runs should take less than three minutes, restart instantly, and be understandable from animation and sound instead of text.”
REQ-012: The visual style shall use sparse geometric space art within a solo developer’s production capacity; exact brief clause: “The visual style should be sparse geometric space art that a solo developer could produce.”
REQ-013: The prototype shall prove that switching gravity anchors creates skillful, predictable movement on one screen; exact brief clause: “The prototype should prove that switching gravity anchors creates skillful, predictable movement on one screen.”
REQ-014: The exact physics model shall remain visibly unresolved until stakeholder confirmation; exact brief clause: “I have not chosen the exact physics model, input accessibility features, target frame rate on low-end devices, or how much randomness each run should contain.”
REQ-015: Input accessibility features shall remain visibly unresolved until stakeholder confirmation; exact brief clause: “I have not chosen the exact physics model, input accessibility features, target frame rate on low-end devices, or how much randomness each run should contain.”
REQ-016: The target frame rate on low-end devices shall remain visibly unresolved until the supported device and browser cohort and measurement conditions receive stakeholder confirmation; exact brief clause: “I have not chosen the exact physics model, input accessibility features, target frame rate on low-end devices, or how much randomness each run should contain.”
REQ-017: The amount of randomness in each run shall remain visibly unresolved until stakeholder confirmation; exact brief clause: “I have not chosen the exact physics model, input accessibility features, target frame rate on low-end devices, or how much randomness each run should contain.”
REQ-018: The intended audience shall remain visibly unresolved because the supplied project context does not declare it.
REQ-019: The quantitative success threshold for skillful, predictable movement shall remain visibly unresolved because the supplied project context declares no representative task, success definition, failure level, percentage, error bound, trial count, or time limit.
REQ-020: The supported browser and device scope shall remain visibly unresolved because the supplied project context does not declare browser families, versions, operating systems, form factors, or minimum hardware.

## Acceptance Register

AC-001: A build inspection passes REQ-001 when the prototype launches as a browser-delivered experience and exposes exactly one primary gameplay action during a run; it fails if installation outside the browser is required or two independent gameplay actions are required supports REQ-001 because the browser game and one-button subjects are directly inspected.
AC-002: A scripted motion scenario passes REQ-002 when at least one moving eligible object changes the satellite trajectory while attached and the satellite can remain in play for ten simulated seconds; it fails if the object is stationary, attachment has no trajectory effect, or survival is impossible supports REQ-002 because moving objects, borrowed gravity, and satellite survival are directly observed.
AC-003: A controlled scene with three eligible objects passes REQ-003 when holding the sole action attaches to the object whose measured satellite-to-object distance is smallest at press time; it fails for any other selected object supports REQ-003 because holding, nearest distance, planet-or-debris eligibility, and attachment are exactly inspected.
AC-004: A deterministic release test passes REQ-004 when the velocity vector immediately after release equals the velocity vector immediately before release within the simulation’s documented floating-point tolerance and the subsequent path departs along that vector; it fails if release applies an impulse or zeroes velocity supports REQ-004 because preserved momentum and the new arc are measured at release.
AC-005: A collection test passes REQ-005 when crossing a data fragment’s documented pickup boundary removes exactly that fragment and increments the fragment count by one; it fails otherwise supports REQ-005 because data-fragment collection is directly observed.
AC-006: A hazard test passes REQ-006 when traversing the documented non-contact skim band adds the documented bonus exactly once and collision does not award that skim bonus; it fails otherwise supports REQ-006 because hazard skimming and bonus points are boundary-tested.
AC-007: A multiplier test passes REQ-007 when the documented qualifying orbital action refreshes or increases the multiplier and expiry follows the documented lapse rule; it fails if qualifying play cannot keep the multiplier active supports REQ-007 because the orbit multiplier’s keep-alive behavior is timed and observed.
AC-008: A drift test passes REQ-008 when every spawned available gravity object has a net starward displacement over the documented observation interval; it fails if any available object does not drift toward the collapsing star supports REQ-008 because object drift and the collapsing star destination are measured.
AC-009: A run-duration test passes REQ-009 when 100 percent of 100 automated runs terminate before 180.000 seconds of simulation time; it fails if any run reaches or exceeds 180.000 seconds supports REQ-009 because the less-than-three-minutes limit is measured exactly.
AC-010: A restart test passes REQ-010 when one restart activation after termination resets score, multiplier, satellite state, and run timer and begins the first new simulation tick no later than the next rendered frame; it fails if a page reload or extra confirmation is required supports REQ-010 because instant restart is operationally observed under the provisional next-frame interpretation.
AC-011: A first-use comprehension test passes REQ-011 when each participant receives no visible instructional prose and can demonstrate attach, release, collection, and run-end recognition using only animation and sound cues; it fails for any participant who must receive visible instructional prose, with the participant cohort and passing proportion still blocked by the unresolved audience threshold supports REQ-011 because animation, sound, and absence of visible instructional text are directly observed without inventing the unresolved quantitative threshold.
AC-012: An asset audit passes REQ-012 when all shipped game-space visuals are geometric primitives or reusable procedural compositions, the art inventory identifies no bespoke illustrated frame sequence, and a solo developer signs off the inventory as producible within the agreed prototype schedule; it fails if any condition is false supports REQ-012 because sparse geometric space art and solo-developer production capacity are inspected.
AC-013: A one-screen movement study passes REQ-013 only when a stakeholder-approved representative anchor-switching task, participant cohort, predictability metric, and numeric threshold have been recorded and met on a single gameplay screen; it fails before those inputs exist or when the threshold is missed supports REQ-013 because skillful predictable anchor switching is tested without inventing its unresolved success threshold.
AC-014: A specification inspection passes REQ-014 when the force model, anchor-selection details, anchor motion, numerical integration, collision handling, excluded forces, gravitational parameters, collision radii, and timestep are all marked pending stakeholder confirmation and none is presented as settled; it fails if any exact physics model is asserted supports REQ-014 because every bundled physics-model choice is checked for visibly unresolved status.
AC-015: A specification inspection passes REQ-015 when supported input devices, hold-versus-toggle behavior, key bindings, control semantics, focus behavior, target sizing, gamepad scope, remapping scope, and claimed conformance are marked pending stakeholder confirmation; it fails if any is presented as settled supports REQ-015 because the complete input accessibility feature set is checked for unresolved status.
AC-016: A performance-plan inspection passes REQ-016 when no numeric frame-rate target is declared until named low-end devices, supported browsers, measurement conditions, sampling duration, and sustained-or-minimum criterion are stakeholder-approved; it fails if an FPS value is asserted earlier supports REQ-016 because frame rate, low-end devices, browsers, and measurement conditions are explicitly gated.
AC-017: A generation-plan inspection passes REQ-017 when no variation amount, randomized-element set, distribution, or seed policy is declared as settled before the replay-variation goal and predictability outcome are stakeholder-approved; it fails if any is asserted earlier supports REQ-017 because per-run randomness remains visibly unresolved.
AC-018: A project-record inspection passes REQ-018 when the intended player population is marked pending stakeholder confirmation and no demographic, ability, experience, or usage cohort is treated as authoritative; it fails if an audience is invented supports REQ-018 because the undeclared intended audience is kept unresolved.
AC-019: A validation-plan inspection passes REQ-019 when no numeric movement threshold is treated as authoritative until the intended audience, representative task, success definition, failure level, metric, and sample plan are stakeholder-approved; it fails if a percentage, error bound, trial count, or time limit is invented supports REQ-019 because the quantitative skillful predictable movement threshold is explicitly gated.
AC-020: A compatibility-plan inspection passes REQ-020 when browser families and versions, operating systems, form factors, minimum hardware, and real-device coverage are marked pending stakeholder confirmation; it fails if a support matrix is presented as settled supports REQ-020 because the supported browser and device scope remains visibly unresolved.

## Interpretation

“Visible instructional text” excludes labels or status exposed to assistive technology; the final accessible representation remains part of the unsettled input-accessibility choice. The prototype cannot claim the movement proof until the stakeholder supplies the audience, task, metric, and threshold required by the acceptance observation.
