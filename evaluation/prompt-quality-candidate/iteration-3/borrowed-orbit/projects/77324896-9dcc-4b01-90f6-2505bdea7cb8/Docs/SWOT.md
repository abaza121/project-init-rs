# Strategic Snapshot

## Strengths

- The one-button attach-and-release loop is compact, demonstrable, and aligned with short browser sessions.
- Momentum, anchor switching, collectibles, skim scoring, and a multiplier create several mastery layers from one action.
- Sparse geometric art and a single screen fit the declared solo-developer production boundary.
- Runs below three minutes and direct restart support rapid iteration during prototyping.

## Weaknesses

- Planning inference (non-authoritative): the mechanic may feel arbitrary until anchor selection, trajectory feedback, and collision boundaries become visually legible.
- Planning inference (non-authoritative): animation-and-sound onboarding may be difficult to validate while the intended audience and comprehension threshold remain undefined.
- Core feel cannot be tuned responsibly until the exact physics model and randomness policy are confirmed.
- Performance cannot be accepted while “low-end,” browser coverage, and frame-rate measurement remain undefined.

## Opportunities

- Planning inference (non-authoritative): deterministic controlled scenes could make physics iterations directly comparable without committing live runs to deterministic content.
- A tiny geometric vocabulary can support strong motion identity, rapid asset reuse, and clear telegraphing.
- Skim bands and multiplier timing can reward confident routes without adding new controls.
- The prototype can isolate whether anchor switching itself is compelling before adding progression or content volume.

## Threats

- Planning inference (non-authoritative): nearest-object attachment may surprise players when two candidates are almost equidistant unless selection is strongly telegraphed.
- A sustained hold can create an accessibility barrier; alternatives could also alter timing skill, so stakeholder choice and parity testing are necessary.
- Unbounded randomness could undermine predictable movement, while too little variation could reduce replay interest.
- Adding accounts, leaderboards, narrative, or a large asset catalogue would dilute the mechanical proof and solo-developer scope.

## Immediate Posture

Protect the one-screen mechanical proof. Resolve the audience, exact physics, input-accessibility baseline, movement metric, browser and device cohort, performance target, and randomness policy before claiming validation or widening scope.
