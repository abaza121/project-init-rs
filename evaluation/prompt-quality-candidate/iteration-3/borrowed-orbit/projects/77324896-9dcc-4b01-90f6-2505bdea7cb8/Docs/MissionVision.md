# Purpose and Direction

## Mission

Create a compact browser game in which a satellite survives through one expressive action: borrowing gravity, releasing with momentum, and chaining readable arcs while collecting data and skimming danger.

## Prototype Purpose

Demonstrate on one screen that switching gravity anchors can produce movement that players experience as skillful and predictable. This proof remains incomplete until the intended players, representative task, measurement method, and passing value are confirmed.

## Vision

A run should feel like drawing a fleeting orbit through a collapsing system: immediately graspable, fast to replay, and deep enough that timing and spatial judgment—not interface complexity—drive improvement.

## Principles

- One action, several consequences: attach, curve, release, collect, skim, and sustain momentum.
- Motion explains the rules: geometry, trails, pulses, and sound communicate state without visible instructional prose.
- Short failure loops: every run ends before three minutes and returns directly to play.
- Honest prototype scope: unresolved physics, accessibility, performance, randomness, audience, support, and proof thresholds stay visibly open.
- Solo-scale craft: reuse a small geometric vocabulary rather than depending on illustration-heavy asset production.
