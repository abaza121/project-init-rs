# Mission and vision

## Mission

Create a one-screen browser prototype that lets a player build and release orbital momentum with a single action, making every gravity-anchor switch legible enough to learn and expressive enough to reward skill.

This mission synthesizes the fifteen product clauses listed individually in the source-clause register. It does not add authority beyond those clauses.

## Vision

Borrowed Orbit should feel like a tiny kinetic instrument: sparse shapes, moving masses, and compact audiovisual cues produce short runs in which the player can see a path, commit, release, and immediately understand the consequence. A successful prototype proves the movement thesis, not a complete commercial game.

## Prototype outcome

The prototype is intended to evaluate three linked questions:

1. Does nearest-anchor hold/release control satisfy R-004 and R-005 consistently?
2. Can data fragments, hazard skims, the multiplier, inward drift, and the collapsing star create a coherent run under R-006, R-007, R-008, R-009, and R-010?
3. After stakeholders define OQ-008, OQ-009, and OQ-010, does T-015 provide evidence sufficient to pass or fail R-015?

The third outcome is currently blocked. It must not be described as proven merely because the prototype is functional or feels promising to its creator.

## Principles

- **Readable causality:** each attach, acceleration, release, and outcome should have a visible cause. This is a design principle supporting R-015, not its pass result.
- **One action, meaningful timing:** complexity should come from when the player holds and releases, not from adding controls (R-002, D-002).
- **Fast learning loop:** a short run and restart should return the player to another attempt quickly (R-010, R-011).
- **Sparse production:** geometry and procedural motion should carry the identity within solo-developer scope (R-013, R-014).
- **Evidence before claims:** research can constrain methods, while stakeholders approve product choices and T-015 determines the movement proof claim.

## Boundaries

In scope is one-screen prototype play, its scoring loop, audiovisual feedback, candidate physics, configuration, instrumentation needed by named tests, and only the settings required by approved accessibility decisions.

Out of scope unless separately authorized are accounts, online leaderboards, social systems, narrative campaigns, multiple levels, monetization, telemetry export to remote services, app-store packaging, and a production content pipeline. These are scope-control recommendations under D-013, not user requirements.
